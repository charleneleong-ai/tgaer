# Leaders pull away; throughput becomes our lever

Between 2026-10-06 and 2026-10-12 nothing new appeared that we could adopt, and one public trace study gave independent support to our throughput thesis. Yi-Chia Chen climbed again to **67.49**, now **10.97 points** ahead of Tufa Labs (56.52, unchanged across its 160th entry). The 40+ tier grew from 5 to 7 teams. No top-10 team disclosed a method, and the best public notebook is a new dfranzen-style copy at **33.22** ([tracker JSON](https://arc.markbarney.net/api/kaggle/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/board); [Kaggle Code tab](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/code)). No usable open model was released: Qwen 4 still has no weights, no repo and no primary date, and every other new release is either far beyond 96 GB or weaker than what we run. The one serving result worth acting on is a community **retrained MTP drafter** for quantised Qwen3.8-Flash-Next. It raised per-position draft acceptance by about 0.04-0.07 and decode speed by **3-8%** at 4-8 concurrent requests ([card](https://huggingface.co/ursuciprian/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-NVFP4-GDN-MSE)). The research that matters most is Hivemind's public trace analysis of the exact stack we run, dfranzen Milestone 2 on Flash-Next. Of the points lost, **97% come from levels never finished**. Unfinished levels end at a median of 14 turns against 6 for won ones, and levels still open after 45-60 actions are later cleared 56-60% of the time. The authors read this as levels running out of time, not stalling ([Hivemind FINDINGS](https://github.com/HivemindEnt/hivemind-arc-agi-3-pub/blob/main/FINDINGS.md)), which is what our throughput replay assumed. The plan therefore holds: final picks of D′ and plain dfranzen, Flash-Next as the final model, and Qwen 4 treated as off the table. The changes are three. Ship the score-neutral throughput levers on their tok/s numbers. Treat the public A/B as a non-regression check rather than a test of the gain, because 8 draws per arm cannot resolve it. And if we build the "controls that already worked" intervention, gate actions with it instead of adding prompt text.

## No model to switch to, and one drafter recipe worth copying

The model axis is closed. The Qwen Hugging Face org added no LLM in the window, only Qwen-Image-2.1-Turbo on 10-09. `QwenLM/Qwen4` returns 404, and vLLM's Qwen 4 fuse-op PR has not moved since 09-01 ([HF Qwen](https://huggingface.co/Qwen); [vllm#53909](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/53909)). The `tiny-random-Qwen4Exp*` stubs that appeared on 10-06 are test fixtures for `qwen4_exp`, the architecture name Flash-Next already uses, so they say nothing about a Qwen 4 release ([stub](https://huggingface.co/hf-tiny-v2/tiny-random-Qwen4ExpForCausalLM)). The other new models are out of reach. Mistral Large 4 is 1.05T parameters, with weights reported (by secondary press) for 10-27 ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/06/mistral-ai-releases-mistral-large-4-le-chonk-a-1-05t-parameter-open-weight-multimodal-moe/)). Reflection's Beam is 501B with no date ([technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/06/reflection-ai-beam-open-weight-model-501b/)). The MiMo-V2.6 updates are refreshes of 310B checkpoints. JetBrains' Mellum2.1 (12B-A2.5B) trails Qwen3.5-9B on its own card, for example SWE-bench Pro 28.0 vs 38.0 ([card](https://huggingface.co/JetBrains/Mellum2.1-12B-A2.5B-Thinking)). The 10-25 cut-off for Qwen 4 is now moot: there is no signal of a release before then, and no evidence that a release would be safe to adopt after it.

**Tracing Thoughts** adds a useful calibration point. In the unmodified PRO-LONG harness on the 25 public games, a local Qwen3.8-27B-FP8 reached **6.8-8.4% RHAE and cleared 34-42 levels**, against 96.9-99.95% and 179-183 levels for Opus 5 ([arXiv 2610.11450](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11450)). Our Flash-Next in dfranzen's harness scores about 42.7 locally on the same public set. The frontier harness does not carry over to our backbone class, so switching harness is not the way to close the gap.

On serving, **Penny Royal 3.0.1** (10-11) is a bugfix-only release. Its fixes include the NVMe-PLE compatibility hashes that made 3.0 reject its own attention backend at load, and it states "the same model and performance defaults" ([release](https://github.com/jpezzulli/sglang-rtxpro6000/releases/tag/pennyroyal-v3.0.1)). If we move to 3.x it should be 3.0.1, after checking that its wheels are cp312-compatible with the pinned image. Upstream vLLM (0.31.0) and SGLang (0.5.21) shipped nothing new. The relevant Qwen4Exp spec-decode and SM12x prefill PRs are all still open ([vllm#61174](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/61174); [vllm#61178](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/61178)).

The actionable finding is ursuciprian's drafter refit. They retrained only the 24 dense BF16 `mtp.*` tensors, using a KL loss against the *served quantised* target's top-20 distribution at 6 chained depths. The training data was the model's own outputs: 3.5M anchors for D1 and 10.7M for D3, emulating the served FP8 drafter KV cache. Live acceptance at positions 1-4 rose from **0.82/0.66/0.54/0.43 to 0.86/0.72/0.60/0.50**. Decode rose **+4.9% to +8.1%** at 4-8 requests on two DGX Sparks, against noise bands of 2.6-3.6%. D3 added a further +3.3% on one box and was inside noise on the other ([card](https://huggingface.co/ursuciprian/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-NVFP4-GDN-MSE)). A second user swapped D1 into a different NVFP4 build and measured **+8.6% median decode** (81.7 vs 75.2 tok/s) on two Sparks at TP=2 ([claidler card](https://huggingface.co/claidler/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-NVFP4-MXFP8-QAD-LMHead-NVFP4-GDNMSE)). The weights themselves will not transfer, because they target a QAD-NVFP4 base on vLLM b12x and SM121, not our Intel W4A16 on Pennyroyal and SM120. The recipe is backend-agnostic, though.

There is one hint that our headroom is larger than theirs. If their per-position numbers are cumulative acceptance probabilities, their original drafter's expected accept length at 3 steps is about 1 + 0.82 + 0.66 + 0.54 ≈ 3.0. Our measured accept length is **2.66** under NEXTN with 3 steps, which suggests our drafter is less matched to ARC traffic (long agentic contexts and repetitive action JSON) than theirs was to a general mix. This is an inference that depends on the two metrics being defined the same way. The same checkpoint's other change, weight-only NVFP4 GDN projections (+4.4-7.8% decode), needs a W4A16 NVFP4 linear kernel that Pennyroyal may not have on SM120. It ranks below the drafter. Neither gain was measured above 16K context or at 10 streams.

## Unfinished levels run out of time, which is the throughput mechanism

Hivemind's public repository holds the most relevant evidence of the week. It replays dfranzen Milestone 2 with Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on 25 public games × 4 passes per arm ([repo](https://github.com/HivemindEnt/hivemind-arc-agi-3-pub)). Three of its numbers bear directly on our plan ([FINDINGS](https://github.com/HivemindEnt/hivemind-arc-agi-3-pub/blob/main/FINDINGS.md)):

1. **97% of lost points are levels never finished**, for example 51.9 vs 1.6 points in one run, so extra actions on finished levels barely matter.
2. **Unfinished levels end at a median of 14 turns (p90 26), against 6 for won levels.**
3. **Levels still open at 45 or 60 actions were later cleared 60% and 56% of the time.** For that reason the authors dropped a forced-RESET watchdog: it would have interrupted many eventual wins.

Together these say the agent usually would solve the level given more turns, and the 9-hour budget ends first. That is the mechanism our scheduler replay simulated. Over three real local eval25 runs, a throughput multiplier moved the simulated score from 50.5 (stock) to 52.1 (+5%), 52.4 (+10%), 54.3 (+20%), 57.2 (+30%) and 59.9 (+50%), with fewer games parked unfinished. The simulation overstates the level (stock sim 50.5 against 42.7 measured), but Hivemind's independent data now supports its direction.

The same repo explains why prompt-level fixes keep failing. Goal choice is mostly not the problem: in 5 of 9 hand-read failed episodes the model stated the right goal and kept it. The failure is **anchoring on the previous level's mechanics**. Failed level-2+ attempts appeal to the previous level 2.0-2.6 times as often as won levels. Tracing Thoughts finds the same failure at frontier scale, where the costliest error is "a hard-coded value carried into a task where it no longer holds" ([arXiv 2610.11450](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11450)). An explicit un-anchoring prompt cut previous-level appeals about 3x, from 0.098 to 0.034 per turn, yet levels won moved only **402 → 414 (+3%, p=0.82)**. A `probe_controls()` sandbox tool fixed one new-mechanic game (lf52 cleared level 2 in 3 of 4 passes against 0 of 4) but lowered the total to **372 (−7.5%)** ([FINDINGS](https://github.com/HivemindEnt/hivemind-arc-agi-3-pub/blob/main/FINDINGS.md)). This matches our own nulls on scheduler, prompt, restart, stall-probe, reasoning-window and replay-simulator changes. Changing what the agent believes does not change how many levels it finishes.

Hivemind also measured the premise of the intervention we were considering. **73-82% of no-op actions (about 6-11 per game-pass) hit a control that had already worked 2-3 times on that level**, and the agent is not told this. Their "trackrecord" arm is queued but has no result yet. Two points shape how we would build it. First, the field has already run a nearly identical information intervention as prompt text: leoprovorov's F002 added a per-level summary of what each key did and scored **18.95 against 25.98** without it ([F002](https://www.kaggle.com/code/leoprovorov/f002-fable-layer-notebook); [F001](https://www.kaggle.com/code/leoprovorov/f001-fable-layer-notebook)). Second, persistent beliefs harden errors. Hivemind's carry-forward notes memory dropped a Duck build from 0.34 to **0.04**, after it "kept a confidently wrong world model for over 100 turns". EvoBelief's large text-environment gains come from rules that **veto actions or change predictions while the actor never sees rule text**, with demotion when counterexamples appear (ALFWorld 66.42 vs 42.54 for a frozen world model on Haiku 4.5) ([arXiv 2610.12789](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.12789)). The trackrecord idea therefore has its best chance as an action-level mechanism: deprioritise or annotate re-tries of the dominant control only after the level changes, with an expiry. It also works mainly by saving turns, so it is a throughput lever as well. Its ceiling is small. At 6-11 wasted actions per game-pass, it is worth roughly a 5-10% turn saving on the levels that matter. That is about the size of one serving tweak, and it costs more engineering risk.

## A powered A/B cannot see a throughput gain, so ship throughput on tok/s

Hivemind measured public-score noise directly. The unchanged public Milestone 2 notebook, submitted on consecutive days, scored **28.49 and then 23.63**, and the pooled run-to-run sd is about **2.4**. By their arithmetic, a +2 point gain needs about **24 hidden scores per side** ([FINDINGS](https://github.com/HivemindEnt/hivemind-arc-agi-3-pub/blob/main/FINDINGS.md)). Our own draws are noisier: plain 28.39/27.86/24.60 and D′ 29.68/29.71/26.60/22.40, a pooled sd near 3. With 8 draws per arm, the standard error of the difference is 1.2-1.5 points. The minimum detectable effect at 80% power is therefore about **3.4-4.2 points two-sided**, or about 3 points one-sided at α=0.1.

Now size the effect we hope to measure. A realistic stack is 256 px (+5% measured), a retrained drafter (+3-8% if the Spark result transfers) and tuned speculative settings, which compounds to roughly **+10-15% throughput**. On the replay that is +1.6 to +3 simulated points. Scaled by the simulation's optimism (42.7/50.5) and by the observed local-to-public ratio (about 27/42.7), it becomes roughly **+1 to +1.5 public points**. That is a third of what the A/B can detect. A +30% stack would come to about +3.6 public points and still be borderline. The planned 8-vs-8 A/B would most likely return "no significant difference" for a real gain, and we have already learned that local RHAE does not predict public score.

The way out is to separate the levers by whether they change outputs. A retrained drafter, speculative-decoding settings and kernel changes are **distribution-preserving**: speculative sampling leaves the target model's output distribution unchanged, so the only effect is more tokens per hour within the 9-hour budget. For these, tok/s at C=10 and at long context is the right measurement, and our local measurement of it is low-noise. They can ship on that evidence plus one public draw confirming the kernel still scores. The 256 px change does alter inputs; it has a measured no-cost local result and the VISTA precedent. "Shorter thinking" is lossy, and our reasoning-window run (24.27) shows how lossy budget cuts can fail. It should be scored, not shipped on tok/s.

Under that framing, the ~21 remaining slots work best as a **non-inferiority check plus a pooled estimate**, not as a test of superiority. Run D′ plus the throughput stack against D′, pooling with the four existing D′ draws. The question the draws answer is whether the stack ever breaks or collapses a run, as our earlier new-slug 0.00 did, not whether it gains 1.5 points. Hivemind's own response to the same power problem is a targeted 6-game × 16-pass local arm with a pre-registered gate. That is also the right instrument for trackrecord, if we build it ([FINDINGS](https://github.com/HivemindEnt/hivemind-arc-agi-3-pub/blob/main/FINDINGS.md)).

## Conclusion

This week's evidence shifts the question from "what does the agent believe" to "how many turns does it get". The independent trace analysis of our exact stack says unfinished levels are mostly cut off, not stuck. Every belief-shaping intervention, ours and the field's, has moved behaviour without moving levels won. And the one new artefact with a measured gain is a speed recipe. That makes throughput the one remaining lever that can be measured and that we control. It also suggests a better use of the scarce public draws: use them to confirm that nothing broke, not to prove a gain they are too noisy to resolve.

The top of the board remains a separate competition. Yi-Chia's steady 3-7 point steps and the silent 40-45 tier come from undisclosed methods that no public harness change reproduces, and that will not change before the 11-02 write-ups. A realistic goal for the remaining 21 days is a dfranzen-family entry that gets 10-20% more turns into the same 9 hours, plus confidence that it scores reliably in the hidden rerun. Chasing the leaders is not.
