# Secret leaders pull away while copies drift upward

Since 2026-10-06 the ARC-AGI-3 Kaggle leaderboard has split into three tiers, and **nothing published in the last five days explains either of the top two**. Yi-Chia Chen jumped from 48.59 to **62.96** on 10-10 and overtook Tufa Labs (55.89 → 56.52). A new 40–45 tier appeared (mtg 45.00, Majkel1337 42.66, NVARC3 40.97), and the number of teams at 32 or above doubled from 55 to 115, almost all of them from 10-09/10-10 submissions ([Kaggle LB](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/leaderboard)). The 32–39 band is still best read as dfranzen copies drawing lucky: two new public notebooks that are byte-identical to dfranzen on the submission path score 32.60 and 31.76. None of the top five teams has disclosed a method, and Tufa has said it will not open-source for Milestone 2. The only new public technical signals are three. First, two dfranzen forks are collecting frontier-model trajectories on community games to fine-tune Qwen3.8-Flash-Next. Second, Son Pham's team showed that **carried-context restarts clear stuck levels and fresh restarts do not**. Third, VISTA reports that **256 px boards match 512 px at 75% fewer image tokens**. No new open model fits 96 GB. Penny Royal 3.0 is the only meaningful serving release, and its +17% headline does not apply at our concurrency. For us, the immediate priority is operational. Our 0.00 came from a new kernel slug, and three new Kaggle failure modes since 10-01 match that pattern. After that, the best remaining bets are spending freed tokens on history (the 256 px run plus a longer window) and measuring level re-attempt on a level-start replay harness instead of full public draws. Public draws cannot resolve 2-point differences before 11-02.

## Our 0.00 matches three new Kaggle failure modes, not a harness bug

Our D-prime plus level re-attempt kernel scored 0.00 in the scored rerun. It was pushed from a new kernel slug, while the proven D-prime kernel scores normally. Three failure modes reported since 10-01 fit this pattern better than a logic error.

The first is the **10-01 image switch to Python 3.13**. Until 09-30 the default image was Ubuntu 22.04 / Python 3.12, and the Pennyroyal v2.5.3 wheelhouse is `cp312`. kozykappa's audited copy states the consequence: "A Kaggle image using Python 3.13 cannot install wheels into Python 3.13". Only "Pin original environment" on a copy of the original notebook fixes it, and "merely importing an `.ipynb` into a new notebook ... does not pin an older Docker image" ([kozykappa](https://www.kaggle.com/code/kozykappa/arc-agi-3-flashnext-duck-audited-submission)). We pin dfranzen's image by sha256 in `kernel-metadata.json`. Nobody has confirmed whether the scored rerun honours a metadata pin on a newly created slug or falls back to the default image. That is the open question in [745654](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/745654).

The second is a **dataset-version race**. Son Pham's team pushed a notebook four seconds before a dataset version finished processing, and it silently ran the previous version's code, while `kaggle datasets status` reported "ready". They now check an `EXPECT_DATASET_BUILD` marker file before the server starts. They also note that Kaggle unpacks uploaded `.gz` files ([level-start-sprint doc](https://github.com/sonpham-org/arc-3/blob/main/docs/2026-10-09-level-start-sprint.md)).

The third is **mount failures** (`ERRORED_MOUNTING_DATASET`, 30 retries exhausted), reported by CPMP on 10-08 ([747438](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/747438)). superstringdev also notes that competition reruns get the game list from the gateway, while ordinary runs use an offline filter ([notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/superstringdev/arc-agi-3-nvfp4-sglang-public)). A code path that only runs in the scored rerun can therefore fail without ever showing up in a save run.

So the resubmission from the proven kernel (ref 57056010) is the right move. Every remaining experiment should ship as a **new version of that kernel, never a new slug**. Each version should carry a build-ID marker and a preflight that fails loudly on the wrong Python, missing inputs or the wrong accelerator, so a 9–17 hour queue slot is not spent producing a silent zero.

## Carried context and smaller boards are the only new levers with evidence

**Level restarts work only when the model keeps its own history.** Son Pham's team built a harness that replays stuck public-game levels from saved checkpoints. A no-context, 30-minute, one-pass control cleared **0–1 of 11** stuck levels, and the result flipped from run to run. A 6-hour carried-context harvest, restoring 70–91 messages and 7–35 retained functions, cleared the stuck level on **9 of 16 lanes, covering 6 of 9 targeted levels**. Their verdict was that "half hour often not enough to clear even the level before" ([level-start-sprint doc](https://github.com/sonpham-org/arc-3/blob/main/docs/2026-10-09-level-start-sprint.md)). These are single passes per arm and the authors call them noisy. Even so, they agree with every history result so far: rellik13's earlier no-context restart was null, and his +8.0 came from history depth ([744792](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/744792)). Our re-attempt v4 already keeps level-start history. Its 50.18 local score looks good against the 42.9 unmodified mean, but only 2 of 12 re-attempts cleared their level, and 6/28 pooled is about the ~25% you get from simply continuing. The mechanism has not shown itself in our data. Son's result points to the likely reason: the RESET-at-80K-tokens trigger hands the re-attempt too little remaining budget. Re-attempts need long wall time with context intact, and a re-attempt fired late in a 9-hour run gets neither.

**Image tokens can be cut before history is.** VISTA's ablation on Opus 5.0 found 4× upscaling (256 px, about 77 image tokens per frame) matched 8× (512 px, about 308 tokens) at 75% fewer image tokens. 2× (128 px) degraded, and 16× added nothing ([arXiv 2610.02200](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02200)). The same paper found a 200K context before compaction was best, while 100K lost only 0.4 RHAE. dfranzen's default upscale of 10 gives 640 px boards, so our MULTIMODAL_UPSCALE 4 run tests exactly the configuration VISTA found free, on a different model. If it holds locally, the freed KV should go into a longer retained window. Our HiCache 160K run (48.63) and Son's controls point the same way. Son's controls ran 12 lanes with HiCache and a ~131K window and scored 55.9–60.6 locally, against our 42.9 mean ([draftB trace diff](https://github.com/sonpham-org/arc-3/blob/main/docs/plans/2026-10-08-draftb-trace-diff.md)). Their team's best public score is 39.24, though, so the local-to-public gap is as large for them as it has been for us.

**Prompt, information and reasoning changes stay negative.** Son's "draftB" short system prompt scored **46.69 against four same-build controls at 55.9–60.6**. It probed more and committed less ([draftB trace diff](https://github.com/sonpham-org/arc-3/blob/main/docs/plans/2026-10-08-draftb-trace-diff.md)). leoprovorov's F002 added a per-level summary of what each key did and scored **18.95** public, against **25.98** for F001 without it ([F001](https://www.kaggle.com/code/leoprovorov/f001-fable-layer-notebook), [F002](https://www.kaggle.com/code/leoprovorov/f002-fable-layer-notebook)). Shehab Anwer's board-view datafication came out at 27.05 vs 24.95 and he calls it "no effect" ([745062](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/745062)). The field has now matched our own nulls (prompt/info changes, and the 24.27 from stripping old reasoning) with independent data. The scheduler picture is unchanged too. Son's team calls D′ a "promising candidate, not proven gain" ([D′ comparison](https://github.com/sonpham-org/arc-3/blob/main/docs/2026-10-05-dprime-slot-priority-vs-franzen.md)). New D′ forks scored single draws of 29.15 and 30.82 ([lwq255](https://www.kaggle.com/code/lwq255/arc3-dprime-guarded), [amatlas](https://www.kaggle.com/code/amatlas/dfranzen-fork-d-prime)), inside the copy distribution. Our own level-prior (20.60) and endgame FIFO (22.56) results say the same: no other scheduler change beats it.

## Serving, quantisation and models offer memory at best, no new brain

**Penny Royal 3.0** (10-10) is the successor to the Pennyroyal 2.5.3 fork we run. The vendor reports +17% single-request decode (186.8 → 218.6 tok/s), but the baseline ran medium reasoning and the new run xhigh. Adaptive MTP "adapts only [at] C=1", and from C=2 upward the draft width is "fixed [at] four" ([release notes](https://github.com/jpezzulli/sglang-rtxpro6000/releases/tag/pennyroyal-v3.0.0)). Its aggregate throughput *falls* from 489 tok/s at four streams to 434 at six ([RESULTS.md](https://github.com/jpezzulli/sglang-rtxpro6000/blob/pennyroyal-v3.0.0/RESULTS.md#penny-royal-30)), which matches our finding that adding streams does not buy throughput. Three parts of the release could still matter to us. The token pool is 2.7% larger. Host Mamba checkpoints are retained per client session, which could make restoring level-start history cheaper. And a scheduler crash on vanished image segments is fixed. All the benchmarks used the OrcaRouter NVFP4 checkpoint, not our Intel W4A16, and the new wheels' Python target must be checked against the 3.13 problem above. Upstream **vLLM 0.31.0** (10-05) absorbed four of lordhansolo's overlay PRs and added Mamba shared-prefix checkpoints, but it removes `VLLM_PLE_CPU_OFFLOAD` ([release notes](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/releases/tag/v0.31.0)). That makes a vLLM port cheaper without changing the earlier conclusion that throughput elasticity is about 0.25. Son's team ships private ARC-tuned MTP drafters, and wkdrbwnd1 published feature-dump notebooks for drafter training ([mtp-featdump-full](https://www.kaggle.com/code/wkdrbwnd1/mtp-featdump-full)). Neither has published acceptance or score numbers.

On the quantisation side, Maximilian228's GPTQ NVFP4 reports **0.00561 expert-output rel-MSE against 0.01252 for NVIDIA's NVFP4**. It loads only in the Strata fork, not in SGLang or vLLM ([card](https://huggingface.co/Maximilian228/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-NVFP4-GPTQ-Strata)). local-inference-lab's CSF-QAD loads in vLLM, but "GPU inference and multimodal quality evaluation have not been run" ([card](https://huggingface.co/local-inference-lab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-NVFP4-MXFP8-CSF-QAD)). Swift 1.5 claims 63.4% fewer thinking tokens ([card](https://huggingface.co/ukisai/Swift1.5-Qwen3.8-Flash-Next)). Our reasoning-strip collapse to 24.27 makes it risky, and its custom licence adds a second eligibility question. Expert pruning stays negative: Son's pruned line "passed every public-set test [but] still scored 8.14 and 3.93 on Kaggle" ([postmortem](https://github.com/sonpham-org/arc-3/blob/main/docs/2026-10-05-lessons-from-tyser-ppo-postmortem.md)).

**No new open model fits 96 GB.** The newest Qwen LLMs are still Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (24 Aug) and Qwen3.8-27B ([HF Qwen](https://huggingface.co/Qwen)). Qwen 4 has no repository, and its open 27B tier is known only from secondary reports of the Apsara announcement ([Yotta Labs](https://www.yottalabs.ai/post/qwen-4-release-date-what-is-known-how-to-prepare-2026)). GLM-5.3-Flash is the only open model with a strong harness result. It is 321B parameters, about 160 GB at 4 bit ([card](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash)). DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash is 763B ([card](https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash)). Community releases are derivatives. One example is an IQ2 Qwen3.8-27B whose AIME25 drops from 96.7 to 79.2 ([card](https://huggingface.co/ConwayResearch/Underdog-Saluki-27B-1.0)). If Qwen 4 27B lands after about 10-25, there is no time to validate it.

## Frontier research converges on verified world models, out of our reach

The new interactive-agent papers report large gains, but almost all of them come with frontier backbones. **Memento 3** compiles a natural-language rulebook into a Python world model. It accepts the model only if cell-exact replay reproduces every observed transition, then plans on it. It reports **RHAE 100.0 on all 25 public games at 44% of human actions** with Claude Opus 5. The rulebook layer cuts actions by 9% in a three-game ablation with one run per game ([arXiv 2610.11794](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.11794)). **PRO-LONG** keeps the full interaction log and lets a coding agent search it programmatically instead of summarising. It reports **+18.0 pp over the base coding agent** and up to 76.1% pass@1 ([arXiv 2607.20064](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.20064)). Prime Agent's persistent-REPL harness lifts RHAE Best@1 from 30% to 95.5% ([arXiv 2608.23552](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23552)). The one open-backbone data point is VISTA with GLM-5.3-Flash at **66.93 RHAE** against 99–100 for frontier models ([arXiv 2610.02200](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02200)). That model is about 2.5 times our model's size and runs without a 9-hour single-GPU budget. All of these numbers are author-reported on public games.

Two lessons transfer. PRO-LONG's "retrieve from the log, don't summarise it" is the history result again, seen from a different angle: our journals and summaries were null, and context depth is what has paid. And the gradient test-time-training papers (ASCENT, [arXiv 2610.05303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05303)) need a verifier on every trajectory. In ARC-AGI-3 the only verifier is a level clear, which is too sparse within one game. The more plausible route to the 40+ tier is **offline**: LamDang's dfranzen fork plays community games with GPT-6.1 Sol and runs an `exp/sft-flash-next` experiment ([repo](https://github.com/LamDang/arc-agi-3-solution-da-fr)), and junpenglao's fork adds community-game trace collection ([repo](https://github.com/junpenglao/arc-agi-3-solution)). Neither has a score. Distilling into a 4-bit MoE, then re-quantising and validating it, in 22 days with day-long queues is not a realistic plan for us. It is, however, the best available explanation of why harness copies cannot reach the top tier.

## Conclusion

The October data changes how to read the leaderboard more than how to tune the harness. The copy band is inflating by order statistics and will keep rising without anyone getting better, so finishing in the 30s with a dfranzen derivative is now ordinary, not a signal. Every public gain above about 40 is undisclosed and probably comes from a different source, such as offline training on frontier trajectories or a fundamentally different agent, that no harness tweak in the public record reproduces. Inside the harness, the independent evidence keeps converging on a single variable: how much of its own history the model keeps when it is stuck. More advice, more structure and more scheduling cleverness have all failed to help. The two changes still worth testing, smaller boards and context-preserving re-attempts, are both ways of moving more of that history into the 96 GB.

The binding constraint for the next three weeks is measurement. Public draws cannot resolve differences of the size we are chasing, and the queue allows roughly one of them per day. Level-start replay is the only instrument that gives enough trials on the levels that decide the score. The most valuable single action is still the least glamorous one: making sure the next scored rerun is not another silent zero.
