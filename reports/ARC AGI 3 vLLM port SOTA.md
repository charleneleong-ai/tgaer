# Skip the full vLLM port; buy memory and probes instead

As of 2026-10-06, the top of the ARC-AGI-3 Kaggle leaderboard has not moved since 10-03. **Tufa Labs leads at 55.89 and Yi-Chia Chen (threerabbits) is second at 48.59**, and neither has disclosed anything technical. Below them, a band of dfranzen-harness copies has drifted up and doubled in size: 55 teams now sit at 32 or above, up from 33. That band is still best explained by draw variance, because **byte-identical dfranzen code has scored anywhere from 27.62 to 34.3 publicly**. The one exception worth watching is Lord Han Solo, who rose from 23.84 to 36.61 with an unpublished configuration. Porting dfranzen's Duck/TAAF harness wholesale onto lordhansolo's vLLM e975732 overlay is **not the best next lever**. The headline 933 vs 588 tok/s comparison sets different harnesses, contexts, image sizes and stream counts against each other. Per stream, SGLang was actually faster (~95 vs ~81 tok/s). Our own runs show that aggregate decode for this harness is compute-bound at ~740–780 tok/s whether 9 or 11 streams are running. At the real per-game token budget, token elasticity is ~0.25, so even a real 20% throughput gain is worth about 1.5 points, well under the ~3.9-point run-to-run sd. The levers that remain are cheaper and aimed at where the score is lost: uncleared levels (95% of the gap) and how much of each game's own history fits in the KV pool. They should be decided on local mechanistic metrics, not on single public draws.

## Two leaders frozen, a copy band inflating beneath them

The 2026-10-05 23:31 leaderboard snapshot shows Tufa Labs at 55.89 (154 submissions, last on 10-03) and Yi-Chia Chen at 48.59; her 21st submission on 10-04 added nothing ([Kaggle LB](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/leaderboard)). The new entries above the old mtg ceiling of 37.54 are **"the last dance" at 39.30** (up from 27.91; the team includes the JKU Linz names Klambauer and Aichberger), mtg at 38.33, **face-of-agi at 37.94** (up from 20.17) and **Lord Han Solo at 36.61** (up from 24.81). Teams at 35 or above went from 6 to 11, and teams at 28 or above from 244 to 402. RTX PRO 6000 queues returned on 10-05 at "~9 hours" waits ([745951](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/745951)), so most teams get only a few scored draws per day.

None of these jumps comes with a disclosure. Tufa's [duck-harness](https://github.com/Tufalabs/duck-harness) repo has no commits since 10-01, and its thread contains only team-up requests ([742801](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/742801)). The forum since 10-03 is about logistics: whether the `qwen-community-1.0` licence on Qwen3.8-Flash-Next is prize-eligible (no host answer; [745079](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/745079)), docker image pinning after Kaggle's 10-01 move to Python 3.13 ([745654](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/745654)), and why dfranzen's notebook page shows 34.3 when his leaderboard entry shows 27.89 ([746010](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/746010)). ARC Prize itself has posted nothing since the 10-01 Milestone 2 announcement ([arcprize.org/blog](https://arcprize.org/blog)). The final deadline is 2026-11-02 ([arcprize.org](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026)).

The most useful new public data is a calibration rather than a technique. **hknight3.0, a copy with zero differing code lines, scored 28.52** ([notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/hknight888/hknight3-0)), and **dfranzen's own page now shows 34.3** on an unchanged 10-03 version ([notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/dfranzen/arc-agi-3-milestone-2-solution)). Together with 27.62, 27.89 and 31.47, unchanged dfranzen code spans at least 6.7 points. Son Pham's first-day copy distribution (mean 25.77, sd 3.93; [745062](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/745062)) predicts maxima of 36–39 across ~400 draws. **The whole 32–39 band is therefore consistent with luck.** This includes our own 28.39 and 27.86, which sit about +0.6 sd above the copy mean. The two new public notebooks above 31 change nothing structural. Affectify's D′ replaces only the slot-priority formula (31.54 on one submission; [notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/shiiin9/affectify-arc-31-54-in-a-single-sub)). sigeward's 31.27 adds teardown and fail-fast wrappers only ([notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/sigeward/arc-agi-3-milestone-2-solution)).

Two jumps deserve a steelman. Lord Han Solo's +12.8 is more than three copy-sds above his own 23.84 baseline. He runs his own harness and the fastest vLLM overlay, and his public notebook is still the 10-01 version, so whatever scored 36.61 is unpublished ([notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/lordhansolo/built-on-tufa-labs-duck-harness-milestone-2)). One reading is that he did exactly the port in question: dfranzen's harness on his server. Another is that he switched to the dfranzen base and drew well, the same story that fits "the last dance" and face-of-agi. The data cannot separate the two. Even if it was the port, that is one draw, and dfranzen's code alone reaches 34.3. **The 16-point gap to Tufa and the 9-point gap to Yi-Chia remain unexplained by any public artefact.**

## The 933 vs 588 tok/s headline compares different workloads

lordhansolo's stack is vLLM nightly `0.29.1rc1.dev573+ge97573215`, plus a hash-pinned 32-file overlay with 9 upstream PR ports, about 16 custom fixes and GDN RecoverSSM. It serves primitive-ai's mixed NVFP4+FP8 checkpoint with a built-in NVFP4 MTP head. The [v3 runtime](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/lordhansolo/vllm-main-e975732-arc3) named in the brief is **not what scored his 23.84**. That run used the earlier [v2 gdn-recoverssm runtime](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/lordhansolo/arc3-vllm-e975732-gdn-recoverssm). The v3 README states that "neither the KV pool delta nor the throughput gain has been measured on the RTX PRO 6000", and his notebook says no v3 run "has beaten that score so far". The best-scoring runtime and the best-throughput runtime are different artefacts.

Tong Hui Kang's side-by-side of the three Milestone 2 Save & Runs is the only comparable serving evidence ([COMPARISON.md](https://github.com/tonghuikang/daniel-franzen-arc-agi-3/blob/main/kaggle/COMPARISON.md)). Read closely, it does not support a server swap for our harness:

| Metric | dfranzen (SGLang Pennyroyal 2.5.3) | lordhansolo (vLLM e975732) | rellik13 (SGLang + HiCache) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Run shape | 10 games, 25 min | 25 games, 14 at once | 25 games, 20 min |
| Steady-state prompt | 60k → 118k | ≤ 81.5k | ≤ 69k |
| Tokens per board image | ~402 (640 px) | ~66 (256 px) | 66 |
| Median decode tok/s | 722 | 976 | 899 |
| Per-stream decode at peak | **~95** | ~81 | ~72 |
| KV pool (tokens) | 1,011,264 | **1,417,100** | 1,004,288 + 48 GB host |
| Job-wide gen tok/s | 588 | 933 | 688 |
| Notebook start to ready | 531 s | 544 s | 615 s |

The aggregate gain comes from 14 rather than 10 streams. Those streams fit because lordhansolo decodes at shorter contexts with board images about six times cheaper. **No evidence shows vLLM decoding a token faster than Pennyroyal at matched context and concurrency.** The median per-stream rate at peak is ~70 vs ~72. Our own Kaggle runs show why more streams will not help dfranzen's harness. At 10 streams, median generation was 743 tok/s with a 0.933 prefix hit. At 12 streams without HiCache, prefix hit collapsed to 0.546 and total tokens fell to 2.69M. With HiCache at 12 streams, prefix hit recovered to 0.933, but total tokens over ~2 h rose only from 4.46M to 4.51M (+1%). Decode at 60–118k context is compute-bound. A bigger KV pool spent on more streams buys almost nothing, and MEMFRAC 0.975 OOMed, so the SGLang pool is already at its ceiling.

The port also carries silent-failure risks, each of which could cost more than any throughput gain:

- lordhansolo caps images at `max_pixels = (64·upscale)²`, which would downscale dfranzen's 640 px boards to 256 px unless it is raised. Raising it grows the vision profiling peak and shrinks the pool.
- The harness stores retained reasoning under `reasoning`. dfranzen calls dropping it a "large regression", and his own diagnostic measured 69 vs 3,670 prompt tokens between the two keys ([WRITEUP](https://github.com/da-fr/arc-agi-3-solution/blob/main/WRITEUP.md)).
- Upstream vLLM align-mode prefix caching on this hybrid GDN model hits only **74–79%**. The better `all` mode is still an unmerged PR ([vLLM #50172](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/50172)), and MTP misaligns the cache boundary on repeated prompts ([vLLM #53504](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/53504)). Only lordhansolo's exact pinned overlay reaches ~91%, and only at his ≤81.5k trim pattern.
- RecoverSSM rejects KV connectors, and the stock CPU-offload connector crashes every 45–80 minutes on this exact nightly at 92–98% pool usage ([vLLM #59768](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/59768)). There is therefore no host KV tier on this path.
- The swap changes three things at once: quantisation (W4A16 → mixed NVFP4+FP8), drafter and server. Any score change would confound all three.

No public run of dfranzen's harness on this runtime exists. A Kaggle kernel search for `e975732` returns only lordhansolo's own notebook.

## Model and kernel upgrades offer memory, not a new model

**Qwen 4 is announced but not released.** No tier has weights, a licence, benchmarks or a date, including the 27B open tier ([Yotta Labs](https://www.yottalabs.ai/post/qwen-4-27b-release-date-specs-hardware-what-is-known-2026)). The rumoured October window traces back to nothing Alibaba has published ([orcarouter](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/qwen-4-leak-vllm-fuse-op)), and the Qwen HF org's newest LLM is still Qwen3.8-Flash-Next ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen)). Flash-Next is itself labelled a Qwen 4 architecture preview (`qwen4_exp`). New releases that fit 96 GB, such as Aleph-Alpha's Kolibri-1 (GPQA-D 84.3 vs Flash-Next's claimed 91.7; [HF](https://huggingface.co/Aleph-Alpha/Kolibri-1)), score below it. The strong new models (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, GLM-5.3) do not fit. Qwen3.8-27B decodes ~1.7× slower at batch 1 and ~19% slower at c=4 on the same card ([h3po bench](https://github.com/h3po/sglang-rtxpro6000)). It edges Flash-Next on one tool-calling bench (91.0 vs 87.9; [MarcoPizeta](https://github.com/MarcoPizeta/flash-next-rtxpro6000-bench)), but there is no ARC evidence for it.

The interesting new artefact is a checkpoint rather than a kernel. [local-inference-lab's NVFP4+MXFP8 QAD](https://huggingface.co/local-inference-lab/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-NVFP4) is ~98 GiB, with the 51B n-gram table also in NVFP4. It is distilled against a BF16 teacher and claims to beat NVFP4 PTQ "while using 9% fewer tokens", though no third party has verified this and no tok/s is published. Two other options are less useful. primitive-ai's checkpoints are at parity with BF16 on generic suites ([card](https://huggingface.co/primitive-ai/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-mixed-NVFP4-FP8)), and dfranzen already found NVFP4 and Intel W4A16 "similar" in quality and throughput. The fastest SM120 decode numbers come from SGLang forks: ~180–193 tok/s single-stream with 3-step MTP ([SSHdotCodes](https://github.com/SSHdotCodes/qwen-3.8-flash-next-pro6000)). They are capped at 2–4 concurrent requests and patched heavily, which makes them a poor fit for a 10-stream kernel. vLLM v0.30.0 shipped Flash-Next-specific fused QSA/PLE kernels, but published no SM120 numbers ([digest](https://localmodelwatch.tsuchitsuchi.com/en/2026/09/22/vllm-v0-30-0-released/)). One caution applies throughout: MTP is a single-stream lever. At c=32 it *lowered* aggregate throughput (805 → 694 tok/s; [kubesimplify](https://blog.kubesimplify.com/running-qwen3-8-flash-next-on-dgx-spark-and-rtx-pro-6000)), and it more than halves KV capacity.

The one model-side change with a measured ARC gain is about memory, not speed. **rellik13 went from 14.49 to 22.53 on the leaderboard with no prompt change.** He switched to FP8 KV and spent the freed memory on keeping 57k rather than 37k tokens of history ([WRITEUP](https://github.com/LohitSiriki/arc-agi-3-milestone2-solution/blob/main/WRITEUP.md)). This is the portable version of lordhansolo's 1.42M-token pool: deeper per-game history at 10 streams, not more streams.

## Throughput buys little because completion is the gap

Three independent diagnostics locate the missing score. skarin's audit of 100 dfranzen trajectories attributes **50.88 of the 53.51 missing points to uncleared levels and only 2.63 to action inefficiency** ([audit](https://www.kaggle.com/code/skarin/arc-agi-3-27-80-lb-100-trajectory-audit)). Six weak games cleared 27 of 196 levels after 3.12M tokens, and "more tokens alone did not establish progress". Makarov finds that in 83% of forks the stuck run had already tried the decisive action type ([743723](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/743723)), so failure comes from committing to the wrong goal, not from lack of coverage. rellik13 adds that the outcome is set by "the first idea it commits to". leoprovorov's token-elasticity fit shows that at the real budget (~181k tokens per game) a 10% token gain buys only ~2.5% score ([handbook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/leoprovorov/fable-astra-play-arc-3-handbook-harness)). On a ~28 base, even lordhansolo's full +59% headline would be worth about 4 points. The +1% we actually measured with HiCache is worth about 0.07 points.

The null list matches our own. Advice text, summarisation, journals that replace history (23 vs 46 levels on 15 hard games), executable world models the model ignores (DF-WM at 29.30, one draw; [notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/vladimiryakunin/df-wm)), fresh-context restarts and REAP pruning were all at or below baseline for rellik13, dfranzen, Makarov and leoprovorov. Our own screens found the same for prompt, harness-information, scheduler, thinking-cap and world-model changes. Scheduler changes cut both ways. D′ has two independent draws, 31.54 and 26.98 ([amatlas fork](https://www.kaggle.com/code/amatlas/dfranzen-fork-d-prime)), a mean +3.5 over the copy mean and inside one sd. Our own prior-fade change took 28.39 down to 20.60. The only untested scheduler idea aimed at the actual gap is the guaranteed bounded first probe in rellik13's harness2: every game gets a first L1 clear, or 40k tokens, or 1,200 s, before value-rate scheduling ([notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/sirikilohit/arc-agi-3-duck-18-1gc-submit)). Our own data says 18 of 25 games score zero, and that is the population this probe targets.

Noise sets the decision rule. With a public sd of ~3.9, detecting a true +3-point gain at 80% power needs roughly 25 draws per arm. With 27 days left and 9-hour queues, that is impossible. **No public A/B we can afford will resolve any lever smaller than ~8 points.** Selection has to rest on local mechanistic metrics: decode tok/s on replayed traces, prefix hit, the share of games with ≥1 level cleared, and levels cleared on the stuck screen. The public score then serves only as a no-regression sanity gate.

## Conclusion

The question has shifted from "which stack is fastest" to "where does the agent lose levels". At this harness's context lengths, serving throughput has become close to irrelevant. Decode is compute-bound, token elasticity sits at a quarter, and the 933 tok/s figure that motivated the port mostly reflects shorter prompts and cheaper images, not a better server. lordhansolo's real advantage is a larger KV pool. That pool is worth having only if it is spent on what rellik13 showed matters: more of each game's own history, so the agent remembers what it has already tried. A pool spent on more concurrent games buys nothing.

The 55.89 leader and the 48.59 runner-up sit 9 to 17 points above anything public. They show that a step change exists, and that it is not a copy, a scheduler tweak, or (on present evidence) a server swap. Given the noise, the sound strategy is to make a small number of mechanistically justified changes, each validated locally on completion rate. Public draws should serve as regression checks, not as the selection signal, and the honest prior is that the final rank will be decided as much by variance on the hidden set as by any of these levers.
