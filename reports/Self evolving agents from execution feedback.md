# Harnesses cracked ARC-AGI-3; nobody measured transfer

The 2026 state of the art in agents that adapt from execution feedback at test time is not a new adaptation algorithm — it is scaffold engineering. Four independent systems (NVIDIA AVO, Tycho, VISTA, Retrodict) report **99.86–100.00 RHAE on the 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games**, and none of them does a gradient update: they externalise an executable or natural-language world model into files that survive context compaction, falsify hypotheses by replaying them over a recorded frame log, and spend environment actions only on plans whose outcome they cannot already compute ([Tycho, arXiv 2607.28287](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28287v1); [Retrodict](https://github.com/ryanbbrown/Retrodict); [VISTA](https://vista-research.github.io/); [NVIDIA AVO](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-avo-reaches-100-on-arc-agi-3-demonstrating-a-frontier-level-general-purpose-architecture-for-long-horizon-autonomous-agents/)). Every one of those figures is **public-set-only, author-reported, and carries the authors' own contamination caveat** — VISTA states plainly that its models postdate the public games and "the private set remains the real test of generalization" — and **no paper, ARC Prize included, publishes how public-set ARC-AGI-3 performance predicts private-set performance**. That missing number is the crux for this reader, because the benchmark's private environments are stated to be "intentionally out-of-distribution relative to the public set" with "limited overlap" in mechanics ([arXiv 2603.24621](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24621v1)). Against that design, a 2.25x local gain producing zero leaderboard movement is not an anomaly: it is the behaviour the split was constructed to produce, and it sits squarely inside the published distribution of self-improvement outcomes — Google's RRSI ablation reports unregularised harness evolution gaining **+3.4 points on its evolve split and +0.6 out-of-distribution, "nearly indistinguishable" from the un-evolved baseline** ([arXiv 2609.24972](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24972v1)). For a model-free explorer the actionable literature is narrow but real: online graph exploration theory says greedy nearest-frontier traversal is already near-optimal, the only ARC-AGI-3 system with a genuine private-set number is a **training-free graph explorer that placed 3rd** ([arXiv 2512.24156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.24156)), and OPINE-World's *ontology error* supplies the one published progress signal that exists before any reward is ever observed ([arXiv 2607.01531](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01531)).

## Test-time adaptation split into two lineages that barely touch

On *static* ARC, gradient test-time training still owns the leaderboard. ARC Prize 2025's private ARC-AGI-2 standings were **NVARC 24.03%** (synthetic data generation plus test-time training on a 4B model), **the ARChitects 16.53%**, and **MindsAI 12.64%** with a test-time fine-tuning pipeline, at roughly **$0.20 per task** for the leading entry ([ARC Prize 2025 Technical Report, arXiv 2601.10904](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10904v1)). Test-time adaptation appears in every >70% ARC-AGI-1 system and is worth **20–30 points over frozen baselines** ([The ARC of Progress, arXiv 2603.13372](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13372v1)). Two numbers in that lineage conflict across ARC Prize's own write-ups and should not be quoted as settled: a secondary summary puts MindsAI at 15.42% against the technical report's 12.64%, and CompressARC appears as both 20% and "20–34%" on ARC-AGI-1 ([results blog](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-prize-2025-results-analysis) vs [technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10904v1)).

On the *interactive* benchmark none of that transfers, and the reason is structural. Gradient test-time training needs a fitting target; ARC-AGI-3 emits nothing until a level clears. The one 2026 method that does true in-episode weight updates, **aTTT**, applies LoRA updates mid-episode with token-level reweighting that downweights repeated n-grams, and buys **+5.0 points success on ALFWorld and +4.9 on SWE-bench Lite at 1.9x the no-adaptation cost** — on environments with immediate per-step feedback ([arXiv 2607.03441](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.03441v1)). That magnitude is the realistic ceiling for in-episode gradient adaptation on a small open model today, and it is purchased with a dense signal this benchmark does not provide.

The in-context RL lineage — algorithm distillation, decision-pretrained transformers, Vintix, DICP — is a source of framing rather than of transferable implementations here. "Unseen environment" in that literature almost always means a held-out task from a parameterised family with a **fixed observation and action interface**; ARC-AGI-3 changes dynamics, changes the semantics of the same five action ids, and hides the goal. Documented failure modes bite exactly there: AD struggles with **novel action spaces**, which spawned a dedicated line of work ([arXiv 2312.13327](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.13327)), and distilled models "inherit the suboptimal behaviors of the algorithms they imitate" ([DICP, arXiv 2502.19009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.19009)). A 2026 survey concedes the open problem — accumulated in-context evidence becomes "stale, misleading, or useful again" as the task shifts ([arXiv 2607.11906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11906)). **No AD/DPT/ICRL result exists on ARC-AGI-3 or any comparable first-contact interactive suite**; the verdict is "untested", not "disproven", but the data requirement (a large corpus of learning histories across a task family) is exactly what a hand-built benchmark of a few dozen distinct games cannot supply.

The harness, meanwhile, is currently worth more than the model. GPT-6 Astra scored **62.7% semi-private under ARC Prize's minimal Standard harness at $26,098, and 99.9% under a Provider Adapter harness that preserves opaque reasoning state between requests and compacts context, at $18,817** — the same model, ~37 points apart, with the cheaper setup also running 3.66x faster on 49% fewer tokens ([ARC Prize: Astra](https://arcprize.org/blog/astra)). NVIDIA reports bare Claude Opus 5 at approximately **30%** versus **100.00** inside AVO, and Schema reports a Claude Code scratch baseline at **42.83%** rising to **98.98%** ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-avo-reaches-100-on-arc-agi-3-demonstrating-a-frontier-level-general-purpose-architecture-for-long-horizon-autonomous-agents/); [Schema](https://schema-harness.github.io/)). Any ARC-AGI-3 number quoted without naming both the harness and the evaluation set is uninterpretable — which is also why the widely circulated aggregator leaderboard, which labels neither, should not be cited ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/arcagi3)).

## Replay verification turns every misprediction into free evidence

The convergent design across the four 100%-class systems is worth reading as a recipe rather than as five separate tricks: externalised durable memory that survives compaction, a typed transition log that distinguishes decision frames from animation frames, hypothesis falsification by replay instead of by acting, cost-gated model building, and plan-with-prediction execution that returns control only on a prediction miss. Retrodict states the load-bearing idea most cleanly — it tests mechanics by "writing python that replays a hypothesis over past frames, where being wrong costs nothing", then commits a queue of actions **with predicted board states** and returns to the model only when a prediction misses, carrying the diff of what went differently ([Retrodict](https://github.com/ryanbbrown/Retrodict)). It reached **99.86% mean RHAE across all 183 public levels at $654 and 660M tokens**, against a baseline run at 98.97% for **$2,722 and 3,640M tokens** — a 4x cost spread at equal accuracy.

Tycho supplies the only clean ablation of the world model itself. Matched policy, Claude Opus 4.8, 25 public games: **79.07 RHAE with no world model, 85.36 with integrated actor-modeling, 88.49 with a builder-on-request orchestrator, 83.07 with automatic trigger-based repair** ([arXiv 2607.28287](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28287v1)). The counter-intuitive row is the important one: the Trigger variant achieved the **highest transition-prediction accuracy at 88.1% and still underperformed**, so "accurate transition prediction alone does not ensure strong gameplay". Optimising a dynamics model for prediction accuracy is the wrong proxy; the useful model is the one that changes the plan, and building it costs actions.

Two 2026 papers do this with per-game program synthesis and are the closest published analogues to a model-free explorer that fits a transition model online. A Codex-CLI-driven agent maintaining an executable Python codebase (`world_model_engine.py`, a planner, a state-IO module) plus three verifiers — the model must reproduce **all** recorded observations, the planner must re-find solutions for already-cleared levels, and a plan executor simulates then executes then compares, halting on the first mismatch — reports **15/25 games fully solved at mean per-game RHAE 58.12%** with GPT-5.5, and **8/25 at 41.29%** with GPT-5.4 ([arXiv 2605.05138](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.05138v2)). Its named failure mode is **premature model commitment**: agents keep refining inside a wrong model rather than considering alternatives. It publishes **no ablations at all** — the authors say simpler variants may match it — so treat its architecture as unvalidated component-by-component. OPINE-World attacks precisely that failure with two cooperating agents over a shared replay buffer, counterexample-guided inductive synthesis of a `transition_function`/`reward_function` pair, and exploration steered by **ontology error**; it clears **20 of 25 games at action-efficiency score 78.4**, versus 14 games / 63.8 for the Codex-style baseline and 12 games / 63.2 for a pretrained continual-learning Vision agent, while **WorldCoder and neural latent world models clear zero games** under budget ([arXiv 2607.01531](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01531)). Its margin is concentrated where it matters: on six games the baseline never clears, the baseline burns **10,874 actions and fails** while OPINE-World clears all six in **2,578 actions** against a human reference of 3,994. Note the comparability gap the notes flag honestly — neither source establishes that "mean RHAE 58.12%" and "action-efficiency 78.4" measure the same thing, so only the games-solved counts (15/25 vs 20/25) should be compared directly.

Around this core, the execution-feedback literature supplies three calibrations. Iterative self-repair works but decays roughly exponentially: multiple independent lines converge on **~3 useful iterations**, a practical ceiling near 5, and evidence that noisy or verbose error feedback makes iterations 3+ actively harmful ([Debugging Decay Index, arXiv 2506.18403](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18403); [arXiv 2502.02928](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02928v2)). Reflective *text* optimisation beats reinforcement learning on sample efficiency when rollouts are expensive — GEPA "outperforms GRPO by ~10% on average while using up to 35× fewer rollouts", explicitly because it consumes the evaluator's text before it collapses to a scalar ([arXiv 2507.19457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457)). And SOAR's hindsight relabelling — converting every failed synthesised program into a valid training pair for the problem it *actually* solved — is the single most transferable trick for extracting signal from an all-zero reward channel; it solved **80% of the public ARC-AGI-1 train set and 52% of the public test set** with open-source models only ([arXiv 2507.14172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.14172)). The counterweight is verifier fragility: on a 49-task sample of SWE-bench Verified, **28.5% of tasks have test suites weak enough that a Docker-verified incorrect patch passes**, and model Pass@1 runs **+14.14 points higher on flagged-hackable tasks (p < 10⁻⁶)** ([arXiv 2606.16062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16062)). The interactive analogue of a weak test suite is a transition or goal model verified only against frames already seen — which is why both executable-world-model papers pair replay verification with an online predict-execute-compare test.

## Under zero reward, an explicit archive beats every scalar bonus

This is where the literature speaks most directly to a model-free explorer, and the answer is unusually crisp: when the environment is deterministic and reward-free, the problem *is* online graph exploration, and the classical bounds are tight. **Nearest Neighbour and hierarchical DFS are Θ(log n)-competitive on arbitrary undirected weighted graphs, with a matching Ω(log n) lower bound that holds even for trees** ([Megow et al. survey](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304397512006445)). Greedy "walk to the nearest state with an untested action" is therefore close to the best defensible default, and no known algorithm beats it on general graphs. Structured cases do better — planar graphs 16-competitive, k distinct edge weights 2k — and the theory is still tightening constants in 2026 ([arXiv 2607.15113](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15113)).

Irreversibility changes the cost model qualitatively. For an unknown strongly connected *directed* graph, exploration cost is governed by **deficiency d, the number of edges needed to make the graph Eulerian**: Albers & Henzinger's Balance achieves **d^O(log d)·m** traversals against lower bounds of **Ω(d²m)** deterministic and **Ω(d²m / log d)** randomised ([Albers & Henzinger](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/fc967a90-a76f-4bc9-8621-5a9ae818962e/content)). The operational reading is that irreversible actions should be *deferred* — explored last from any state — so the explored subgraph stays as close to Eulerian as possible. The applied cost of not doing this is measured: the ARC-AGI-3 graph explorer's officially submitted run solved **12 private levels instead of its median 16**, because a reset-inducing action appeared as a self-edge from the start node and the agent kept re-selecting it ([arXiv 2512.24156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.24156)). Note also the warning from RL theory that determinism alone buys nothing: there is a class of **deterministic** systems with linear Q-functions where any reward-free algorithm needs **Ω(2^H)** exploration samples ([NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/ce4449660c6523b377b22a1dc2da5556-Paper.pdf)). Determinism helps only if you exploit *model-based* structure.

That same paper is the strongest existing evidence for the architecture this reader already runs. Its explorer is **training-free with no reward term whatsoever** — frame segmentation, status-bar masking, state hashing as node id, a directed state→action→state graph, and a policy of shortest path to the nearest untested action at the current salience tier. Results: **median 30 of 52 levels across six games (median 16 private, 14 public) over five 8-hour runs, 3rd on the private leaderboard, "substantially outperforming frontier LLM-based agents"**. The budget-matched ablation at 4,000 interactions is the decisive table: a random agent solves **9** levels, a **GPT-4.1 + DSL code-writing agent solves 5 — worse than random** — untested-action preference without a graph solves **4**, and the complete graph explorer solves **19**. The graph, not the perception, is where the gain sits. Its stated failure modes are honest: cost grows with state-space size, degrading on later levels of ft09 and ls20, and the method "would fail under" stochasticity or partial observability.

By contrast, every classical intrinsic-bonus headline is structurally unavailable here. Pseudo-counts, RND and Go-Explore all report their numbers from **millions to billions of frames across millions of episodes with free resets**, and Go-Explore's specific published advantage was measured *because* it could restore game state: "this optimization allows us to solve the first level **45× faster** than by replaying trajectories" ([Uber AI](https://www.uber.com/us/en/blog/go-explore/)). A method whose win is "45x faster than walking back" is reporting a benefit a no-reset agent cannot have. The Montezuma literature is also internally inconsistent — the Uber blog reports mean **469,209 with domain knowledge** and **35,410 without**, against a prior state of the art of 11,347, while a secondary summary circulates "Go-Explore 43,000 (2021)"; these are different configurations and possibly different papers, and the Nature 2021 table was not verifiable, so **no single canonical Go-Explore Montezuma figure should be quoted**. What survives the no-reset constraint is not the return mechanism but the **archive and frontier bookkeeping** — which is exactly what the graph explorer reimplements.

Bonus-driven methods additionally have named mechanisms for degrading *below* systematic. A 2026 survey of intrinsic motivation lists **noisy-TV distraction, generalisation failure across tasks, vanishing bonuses that "prematurely halt productive exploration", and episodic-reset dependency** ([arXiv 2609.17325](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.17325)); multi-agent work documents **coordination de-synchronisation**, where agents "gradually exhaust their intrinsic motivation to revisit these critical locations" ([Springer 2026](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-19105-2_3)); and Go-Explore's own diagnosis names **detachment** and **derailment**. Vanishing bonuses and detachment are specifically diseases of storing exploration state in network weights. A graph cannot vanish or detach; it can only grow, and under a hard single-life action budget that monotonicity is worth more than sample efficiency.

The one genuinely new zero-reward signal is OPINE-World's **ontology error**. Each object transition reduces to a coarse effect signature, filed into a row keyed by (type, action, local context) with counts; a symmetric Dirichlet prior gives a posterior mean per row, and two normalised entropies — type uncertainty over the object partition and row uncertainty over effect signatures — combine by noisy-OR into per-object error `η_i = 1 − (1−U^type)(1−U^row)` ([arXiv 2607.01531](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01531)). Because it is uncertainty over a *typed partition* rather than over pixels, it avoids the noisy-TV pathology, it is defined on step one before any reward exists, and it doubles as an allocation rule: OPINE-World "avoids spending thousands of actions on a game it cannot yet model" while the baseline exhausts its budget on six such games. Two honest limits: the method assumes **Observable-Markov determinism** and under hidden state "the ontology error … has a floor it cannot drive to zero" — relevant given this reader's finding that a field_box blinds the state signature on five stuck games — and the paper's planner plans "to reward from the entry states of the levels already cleared", so **whether its goal flag is inferable with zero clears anywhere in a game is not established**. That is the precise hole in the literature for a game that will never emit a reward.

## Dev-set gains are bimodal, and a plateau proves nothing

The reader's headline result — local RHAE 0.1561% and 0.3520% both scoring 0.13 on the leaderboard, with 0.030pp seed-to-seed noise — is not an outlier in this literature. It is one of two modes. RRSI's ablation is the cleanest paired table published: unregularised harness evolution reaches the **best evolve-set score (92.8, +3.4) and the worst out-of-distribution average among evolved variants (40.3, +0.6, "nearly indistinguishable from H₀")**, while fully regularised RRSI posts the **smallest evolve gain (90.5, +1.1) and the best OOD (43.6, +3.9)** at 36% fewer policy tokens ([arXiv 2609.24972](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24972v1)). Within its main table the anti-correlation is explicit: Meta-Harness gains +3.6 on the evolve split and +0.3 to +1.5 OOD, while two published methods — **AHE and TTHE — are net negative OOD (down to −2.5) while positive in-distribution**. EvoAgentBench finds the same shape in memory and prompt methods: **Memento −2.4% average with a −36.3% catastrophic cell; ReasoningBank positive on average with six negative per-domain cells; GEPA +1.2% to +5.7% average with a −12.3% cell** ([arXiv 2607.05202](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05202v1)).

Transfer does happen, but in a recognisably different regime. The Darwin Gödel Machine's agent evolved on SWE-bench scored **28.9% on held-out Polyglot against a 14.2% baseline**, and vice versa 24.5% vs 20.0%, never having accessed the alternate benchmark ([arXiv 2505.22954](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954)). AIDE² accepted **7 rewrites out of a 100-node trajectory** and reports its largest gains on the out-of-distribution benchmark ([arXiv 2609.26457](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26457)). SkillBoost's LiveMath-optimised skill transferred to OlympiadBench at **+6.0 and +14.0 points** across two backbones where human-written baseline skills gave 0–3 ([arXiv 2607.26643](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26643v2)). Where transfer works the OOD-to-dev gain ratio is roughly 0.3–1.0 and sometimes above 1; where it fails the ratio is zero or negative. **There is no middle regime of small-but-reliable transfer** — which means a dev gain gives almost no prior on which mode you are in. The structural difference in the transferring cases is that selection was made against a signal **decoupled** from the inner optimisation loop, or across a domain or backbone boundary. The failing cases selected on the signal they optimised. Every 2026 result in this paragraph is author-reported; **none has an independent reproduction**.

On the specific question of whether a broad, stable sweep plateau is evidence of robustness, the literature says no, and says it directly. The hyperparameter-optimisation overtuning study states that flat or plateau regions in a validation landscape are **not necessarily evidence of robust generalisation**, because optimisation "can exploit shallow variations in validation performance that don't reflect test set behaviour", so "landscape flatness alone is insufficient" ([arXiv 2506.19540](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.19540)). RRSI's algorithm treats a plateau as a **stall signal to redirect search** — `σ_t = 1[Ŝ_t − Ŝ_{t−w} ≤ δ]`, triggering reserved candidate slots for never-touched components — not as a robustness indicator. A plateau is a statement about the variance of the dev-set estimator, not about the mechanism; a flat region is equally consistent with "the parameter doesn't matter" and "25 games cannot resolve it". Given that ARC Prize's own harness testing found **one model scoring 97.1% on one public environment and 0.0% on another with the same harness** ([arXiv 2603.24621](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24621v1)), the second reading is likelier. The notes are also right that the reader's exact observation — broad stable plateau, zero transfer — appears **novel as a reported finding**; no paper frames it that way, so it should be claimed as an original datapoint rather than cited.

The apparent counter-argument does not survive inspection. Roelofs et al.'s meta-analysis of over 100 Kaggle competitions found "little evidence of substantial overfitting" from repeated holdout reuse — but it measures adaptive overfitting to a **same-distribution** holdout, whereas ARC-AGI-3's private set is out-of-distribution by construction, and its own stated boundary conditions are **small public test sets and small datasets**, where "practitioners with access to repeated feedback on limited test data can more easily adapt models to noise" ([NeurIPS 2019](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/file/ee39e503b6bedf0c98c388b7e8589aca-Paper.pdf)). At 25 environments, Roelofs predicts overfitting here rather than arguing against it.

Three independent multipliers each suffice to explain zero leaderboard movement from a real local gain, and none requires the local gain to be fake. First, distribution shift: the private mechanics are disjoint by design, the public set was explicitly demoted "from a training resource to a demonstration interface", and **"the official leaderboard will never report public set scores of any system"** ([arXiv 2603.24621](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24621v1)). Second, metric shape: RHAE per level is `min(1.0, h/a)²` against the **second-best** human action count, weighted with later levels heavier — so gains measured in levels cleared can be near-orthogonal to the scored quantity, and sub-human efficiency is penalised quadratically. Third, estimator variance on 25 samples with documented within-set bimodality. The single most valuable missing number in this entire literature is the one nobody publishes: **how public-set ARC-AGI-3 performance predicts private-set performance**. ARC Prize does not report it, no third party publishes a paired public/private figure for the same agent, and ARC's reports carry no Kaggle-style shakeup statistics (public-to-private rank correlation) either.

Two regularisation results are worth adopting on their own merits. RRSI calibrates its acceptance floor by **evaluating the unchanged base harness repeatedly** and taking the empirical spread as δ, then accepts only candidates clearing `S* − δ`, with **no statistical hypothesis test** — calibrated δ came out around 1.7% in coding and 0.43% in the agentic workspace, an order of magnitude apart, so δ must be measured on your own harness rather than copied. And a compression result gives a cheap transfer discriminator independent of any held-out set: **32-token reproducers matched or exceeded the unconstrained explorer on 38 of 41 improvement checkpoints (92.7%)**, replacing the numerical validation score with **a single bit** of better/worse feedback still matched holdout performance on all 8 datasets, and under deliberate exploitation the compression test flagged validation-specific gains at **100% sensitivity and 91% specificity** ([arXiv 2606.11045](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11045v1)). If a change can be stated in a few tokens it is a mechanism; if stating it requires enumerating dev-set cases it is a fit wearing a mechanism's clothes.

## Ranked adoption candidates for a model-free explorer

The ranking below weights three things: whether the idea has evidence from a genuinely held-out set, whether it survives the no-LLM-in-the-loop constraint, and whether it attacks a mechanism this reader's own measurements have already identified as live. Note one hard gate on everything in the LLM column: ARC Prize 2026 specifies **no internet access during evaluation** and requires open-sourcing for prize eligibility ([competition page](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026/arc-agi-3)), so any frontier-API harness is out for the scored track regardless of its public-set RHAE.

### No LLM required — adopt these first

| # | Candidate | Evidence class | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stop selecting on local RHAE; gate every accepted change on an incumbent−δ floor with δ measured by re-running the unchanged baseline** | Author-reported (RRSI), plus reader's own 0.030pp | The reader's measured 0.030pp noise *is* δ. RRSI's ablation shows acceptance-side constraints are worth −2.6 OOD points when removed; the anti-correlation between dev and OOD gain means an ungated loop preferentially banks local artefacts ([arXiv 2609.24972](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24972v1)) |
| 2 | **Type irreversible / reset-inducing transitions as first-class graph edges and defer them from any state** | Genuine private-set evidence | Directed exploration cost is governed by deficiency d, bounded `d^O(log d)·m` above and `Ω(d²m)` below ([Albers & Henzinger](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/fc967a90-a76f-4bc9-8621-5a9ae818962e/content)); the concrete cost of not doing it is the graph explorer's **16 → 12 private-level regression** from a reset self-edge ([arXiv 2512.24156](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.24156)) |
| 3 | **Replay-based falsification: fit a transition model from the recorded state-hash graph and answer "what if" offline instead of by acting** | Public-set only, but architecturally model-free | Retrodict's "being wrong costs nothing" is the highest-leverage idea for an action-efficiency metric, and its implementation is a frame log plus Python — no LLM needed to *replay*, only to *propose* ([Retrodict](https://github.com/ryanbbrown/Retrodict)). Because RHAE squares the ratio, a 2x action overrun costs 75% of a level's score |
| 4 | **Ontology error as a direction and a stopping rule: Dirichlet effect-signature counts over (object type, action, local context), entropies combined by noisy-OR** | Public-set only, author-reported | The only published progress signal defined before any reward is observed, and it doubles as budget allocation — OPINE-World clears six games in 2,578 actions that the baseline fails in 10,874 ([arXiv 2607.01531](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01531)). Directly complements the reader's existing finding that click effects are ~99% pure by (colour, size) |
| 5 | **Budget actions per level against the human median rather than globally** | Metric structure, from the benchmark definition | RHAE is `min(1.0, h/a)²` with later levels weighted heavier ([arXiv 2603.24621](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24621v1)), so an unbudgeted deep-level overrun destroys the score the shallow levels earned. This is the mechanical explanation for capturing only a fraction of solved levels' nominal worth |
| 6 | **Keep all exploration state in the archive; never learn an exploration prior into weights** | Genuine cross-agent evidence on this benchmark | Vanishing bonuses and detachment are diseases of weight-stored exploration state; and OPINE-World's Vision comparison is close to a natural experiment — an agent that **pretrained on the public games and was then scored on them** still cleared 12/25 versus 20/25 for a weightless online method ([arXiv 2607.01531](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01531)) |
| 7 | **Compression and one-bit discipline on the tuning loop itself** | Author-reported, controlled experiment | 32-token reproducers matched the unconstrained explorer on 92.7% of checkpoints and a single bit of feedback matched holdout on all 8 datasets ([arXiv 2606.11045](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11045v1)). Feeding a full 25-game score vector into the loop is overfitting capacity with no measured upside |
| 8 | **Cap per-hypothesis repair at ~3 rounds, then re-propose rather than patch** | Author-reported, converged across several papers | Debugging gains decay roughly exponentially with ~3 useful iterations and noisy feedback actively harming iterations 3+ ([arXiv 2506.18403](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.18403)); the 2026 direction is branching search over repairs, not longer chains ([GraphAHA, arXiv 2609.12757](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.12757)) |

Nearest-frontier traversal itself needs no adoption argument — it is what the reader already runs, and online graph exploration theory says **Θ(log n) is asymptotically optimal among known algorithms for general graphs**, with a matching lower bound. The honest framing is that the traversal layer is close to done; the remaining headroom is in the model layer (items 3 and 4) and the selection discipline (items 1 and 7).

### Needs an LLM in the loop — defer, or run offline only

Program synthesis of a per-game `transition_function`/`reward_function` under CEGIS is the strongest-performing architecture published (**20/25 games**), but it requires an LLM synthesiser at runtime and would have to ship a local model inside the kernel to satisfy the no-internet rule. The four 100%-class harnesses (AVO, Tycho, VISTA, Retrodict) cost **$654 to roughly $2,986 per public-set run** with frontier APIs and are unavailable for the scored track; their value here is as a design template, not as a baseline to chase. Intelligent Go-Explore's foundation-model notion of interestingness needs both an LLM and the ability to return to an archived state ([arXiv 2405.15143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15143)). GEPA-style reflective harness evolution and RRSI's regulariser suite are LLM-proposer methods, but they run **offline against your own harness code** — which makes RRSI's leakage critic (reject diffs encoding game-specific names or constants before spending evaluation budget) and its Lasso-style pruner (delete components with no strictly positive recent gain) adoptable as an offline development discipline even with a model-free runtime. SOAR's hindsight relabelling is the one LLM-lineage idea with a model-free analogue worth building: every synthesised transition model that failed to explain game A but exactly explains a sub-mechanic of game B is still a valid labelled example for B.

## Conclusion

The most useful thing this literature offers is not a technique but a reclassification of the reader's own result. Two local RHAE figures 2.25x apart mapping to an identical leaderboard score is the *designed* behaviour of a benchmark whose authors demoted the public set to a demonstration interface and stated they would never score it — and it is simultaneously the modal outcome of unregularised self-improvement across three independent 2026 studies. That reframes the engineering question from "why didn't my gain transfer" to "what would I have to measure to know in advance", and the answer the literature converges on is uncomfortable: nothing you can compute from the 25 games. Dev-set gain and out-of-distribution gain are close to bimodal with no reliable middle, so the only signals that carry information are structural — was the change selected against something decoupled from what it optimises, does it compress to a few tokens, does it install a mechanism or enumerate cases. A public-set plateau, however broad, is a statement about estimator variance.

Where that leaves a model-free explorer is better than it sounds. The 2026 winners are frontier-LLM harnesses whose public-set numbers are unfalsifiable by construction and whose costs disqualify them from the scored track; the only ARC-AGI-3 system with a genuine private-set placement is a training-free graph explorer that beat a GPT-4.1 code-writing agent by nearly 4x on a matched budget, and beat random by more than 2x. The transferable part of the harness recipe is not the model backend but three components that survive stripping the LLM out: an append-only typed transition log, falsification by replay rather than by acting, and a typed model-uncertainty score that decides where the marginal action goes and when to abandon a game. Two things are genuinely unknown and should be treated as open research rather than engineering risk — whether a goal predicate is inferable with zero observed clears anywhere in a game, which no paper establishes, and how public ARC-AGI-3 performance relates to private, which nobody has published. Given the second gap, the highest-value experiment available is not another sweep; it is calibrating δ on the 25-game set and checking whether the plateau's width is larger than it.
