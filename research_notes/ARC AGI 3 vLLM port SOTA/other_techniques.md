# Non-model, non-serving techniques with measured gains on ARC-AGI-3 (offline, open-weight), as of 2026-10-06

Scope: ARC-AGI-3 only, techniques other than base-model choice and serving/quantisation. Target: dfranzen Milestone 2 notebook (Duck/TAAF harness, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, one RTX PRO 6000, 9 h, 110 games, offline). Noise reference throughout: identical dfranzen copies score mean 25.77, sd 3.93 on the public LB ([Son Pham, 745062](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/745062)). Any single-run delta under ~8 points is not distinguishable from noise at 90% confidence by that author's estimate.

New since the 2026-10-04 sweep (detailed below): skarin's 100-trajectory audit (completion, not efficiency, is the gap), the D' scheduler priority (31.54 and 26.98 from two independent submissions), the Fable-layer handbook (token-elasticity curve; replacement journal halved levels; the 31.73 on that page is an *unchanged* copy), rellik13's new "harness2" notebook, the DF-WM world-model fork scoring 29.30 (one run), Nick Pellegrin's 3-run public-25 numbers, Lord Han Solo moving 23.84 → 36.61 on the LB (undisclosed), and the LB top-12 snapshot of 2026-10-05.

## Q1. Which techniques have controlled, multi-run evidence of gains on ARC-AGI-3?

### Takeaway
No non-model, non-serving technique has a controlled multi-run public-LB gain larger than the ~4-point run-to-run sd. The largest measured single-harness gains are all "spend more memory/tokens on the agent's own history" (rellik13 +8.0), "make the action space and feedback correct" (UNDO, no-op guards, correct game-over text, animation frames, 10x board upscale), and "redistribute compute between games" (priority scheduler, D'); each is one or two runs, and most are already in the dfranzen notebook. The one new candidate since 10-04 with two independent LB samples is D' slot priority (mean 29.26 vs 25.77 copy mean, n=2, inside noise).

## Q2. What did teams report as null or negative?

### Takeaway
The null list keeps growing and is consistent: anything that adds advice text, replaces history with summaries, or adds a world model/planner that the model must choose to use, scores at or below baseline. New since 10-04: a history-*replacing* journal halved cleared levels (23 vs 46 on 15 hard games), and the DF-WM executable world model scored 29.30 on one run (inside the copy band, so not a demonstrated gain).

### Cited Findings
- rellik13 failures: advice text (6.42 → 4.16; level-start advice 13.40 → 9.98); per-action "what changed" notes; level-start comparison block; fresh-context restart of a stuck level; T=1.0 vs 0.6; proven/refuted ledger ("The agent never used it"); REAP 25% expert pruning ("More room, worse play"); higher-resolution images; Swift 1.5 (no better per token, ~35% slower). He also held back priority scheduling because "games stagnate in their later stages ... moving time between games would mostly move stagnant time". — [rellik13 WRITEUP](https://github.com/LohitSiriki/arc-agi-3-milestone2-solution/blob/main/WRITEUP.md)
- dfranzen nulls: structured world-model updates, summarisation (inserted or replacing), resume-prompt variants, composite animation images, stronger step-verification hint, repeated-state guards, remembered no-op/fatal-action guards, death-ledger guidance, "pace adjustment and an empirical continuation lookup" in the priority rule. Removing retained reasoning from history was a "large regression". — [dfranzen WRITEUP](https://github.com/da-fr/arc-agi-3-solution/blob/main/WRITEUP.md)
- Makarov (single model, single harness family, layers isolated, mean of several baselines): consensus between sampled answers, a reasoning-discipline block, higher-resolution boards, and an executable world model with replay validation and search planning all "at or below the baseline". The model "largely ignored" the world model; offline its simulators were rarely better than a "nothing changes" baseline on unseen transitions. Identical builds: 2.60 vs 1.45 back to back (first level cleared in 15 vs 9 games); full-length spread 4.5 to 9.4. — [Makarov 743723](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/743723)
- Scott Le Grand: "Most enhancements to the harness were net negative"; he "failed to find any discriminant that can truncate or reset a solver on the way to wasting everyone's time"; judged changes by best of 3 runs. — [743723](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/743723)
- **New**: leoprovorov's first idea, "a journal that replaced the history, finished with 23 cleared levels against 46 for the unchanged harness on the same 15 hard games". His follow-up Fable layer (indicator notes after 15k tokens on a level, pinned level log after 30k, step-budget alert) is shipped but **not yet scored**; the 31.73 shown on his page is "an unchanged copy of the open notebook". — [leoprovorov Fable & Astra handbook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/leoprovorov/fable-astra-play-arc-3-handbook-harness)
- **New**: DF-WM (dfranzen + `SimFrame`, `check_sim`, BFS `search`, `act_checked`) shows 29.30 best public score, one submission. — [vladimiryakunin DF-WM](https://www.kaggle.com/code/vladimiryakunin/df-wm); Kaggle kernel list 2026-10-06
- Fine-tuning / STaR / RL at Flash-Next scale: null or infeasible on one card (blakewest SFT "no meaningful improvement"; Scott Le Grand +2 public, no hidden transfer). — prior sweep, [743952](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/743952), [742835](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/742835)
- Our own null results (from the brief and repo memory): prompt changes, win records, thinking cap, scheduler priors, world-model toolkit.

### Inferences
- DF-WM's 29.30 is +0.9 sd over the copy mean on one draw; with Makarov's direct evidence that the model ignores such tools, it should not be read as a world-model win.
- The two "history" results point the same way from opposite sides: adding history +8.0 (rellik13), replacing history with a journal -50% levels (leoprovorov). Any memory technique must be additive and token-cheap.

### Gaps
- No ARC-AGI-3 report isolates level-restart policies beyond rellik13's "fresh context bought nothing" (one run, no number).
- No report tests best-of-N runs or ensembling across runs inside one 9-hour kernel (see Q4 inference).

## Q3. Papers or posts since August 2026 analysing failure modes with quantified fixes

### Takeaway
The quantified diagnostics all say the same thing: the gap is level completion driven by early goal judgement, not action efficiency, memory, or coverage. skarin's audit of dfranzen's own 4x25 run attributes 50.88 of the 53.51-point gap to incomplete levels and only 2.63 to action inefficiency. Makarov finds the stuck run had already tried the decisive action type in 83% of forks. Papers with large gains (OPINE-World, Continual Harness, DreamTeam, PRO-LONG/Fable) all use frontier API models and do not transfer.

### Cited Findings
- **skarin audit (new, 10-04)** of dfranzen's v3 public artifact (4 replicates x 25 public games in one shared 8h11m job, 17.50M generated tokens): replicate means 45.59 to 47.46 (mean 46.49), but single games swing from 0 to 100 (m0r0) and 3.57 to 100 (tn36). Weighted completion ceiling averages 49.12 vs actual 46.49, so "50.88 points from incomplete levels and 2.63 from action inefficiency after accounting for completion". 33/100 wins but 23/100 perfect scores (cd82 wins all 4 replicates yet scores 58.62 to 99.42). Six weak games complete 27/196 levels with zero full wins in 24 trajectories after ~3.12M tokens; sk48 never clears level 1; bp35 and lf52 stop after one level in every replicate. "More tokens alone did not establish progress." skarin's own copy: 27.80 then 21.01 on rerun of the identical version. — [skarin 100-trajectory audit](https://www.kaggle.com/code/skarin/arc-agi-3-27-80-lb-100-trajectory-audit)
- **Token elasticity (new)**: leoprovorov fits the bang1850 "sovereign agent" 13-point curve (public-25 score vs generated tokens per game). Below ~60-70k tokens/game, elasticity is 1.5-1.7; near the estimated real budget (626 tok/s x 31,800 s / 110 games ≈ 181k tokens/game) the last two segments are 0.27 and 0.24. A 10% token gain buys ~2.5% score at the real budget. — [leoprovorov handbook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/leoprovorov/fable-astra-play-arc-3-handbook-harness)
- leoprovorov observation (n=10 copies of sk48 level 1, not an experiment): copies that never lost an indicator mark cleared in 21-29k tokens, those losing 1-2 marks needed 44-136k, the one losing 4 spent 170k and failed; Spearman ρ=0.93, permutation p=0.0004. He also measures per-copy sd of 0.97 levels, so 10 copies per arm resolve ~0.87 levels and 30 copies resolve 0.5. Fable 5 cleared sk48 L1 in 32 actions vs human 61. — [leoprovorov handbook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/leoprovorov/fable-astra-play-arc-3-handbook-harness)
- Makarov: aligning stuck and successful runs state by state, "in 83% of the forks the stuck run had already tried the action type that later turned out to be decisive"; harness-visible signals (repeats, stalls, move counts) predict it only late. — [743723](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/discussion/743723)
- rellik13: "The same agent clears a level in one run and is stuck on it for an hour in the next, depending on the first idea it commits to. The first 30 minutes of a run say little about the result." — [rellik13 WRITEUP](https://github.com/LohitSiriki/arc-agi-3-milestone2-solution/blob/main/WRITEUP.md)
- ARC Prize analysis of GPT-5.5 / Opus 4.7 (2026-05-01): three qualitative failure modes ("true local effect, false world model"; "wrong level of abstraction from training data"; "solved the level, didn't learn the game"), no quantified fixes. — [ARC Prize blog](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-agi-3-gpt-5-5-opus-4-7-analysis)
- Frontier-model papers (do not transfer, no frontier API in the kernel): OPINE-World raises Claude Opus 4.8 RHAE "from 1.5% to 78.40%" with programmatic world modelling ([arXiv 2607.01531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01531)); Continual Harness (Gemini 3.1 Pro) 20.54% public at $774, 62% of executed actions from saved skills, 1.48x human actions on completed levels, single run ([Seth Karten](https://sethkarten.substack.com/p/continual-harness-an-efficient-self)); DreamTeam 36% → 38.4% RHAE public with 31% fewer actions (444 vs 643), two runs ([arXiv 2605.09650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09650)); PRO-LONG / Fable 5 is the agent leoprovorov studied ([arXiv 2607.20064](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.20064)).
- Small open model evidence: AERA (Qwen2.5-0.5B, explore/verify/plan) reaches public RHAE 0.2116 (4/25 solved) and 0.30 private; authors note every public solve "is reachable through non-intelligent strategies". — [arXiv 2605.25931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25931)
- arc3cb (criticaldata) proposes retrodiction, plan queues with expected boards and playbook memory for open-weight models via Cerebras; as of 2026-08-22 no campaign results were published. — [criticaldata/avo-qwen-arcagi3](https://github.com/criticaldata/avo-qwen-arcagi3)

### Inferences
- Combining skarin (completion is ~95% of the gap) with Makarov (83% of failures are wrong goal commitment, not coverage) and rellik13 (outcome set by the first idea) gives one target: more independent goal hypotheses per stuck level, not more information or more efficiency. The cheapest offline form is diversity at the level, e.g. a second context that does not inherit the first's committed hypothesis. rellik13's fresh-context restart was null on one run, so this needs a better trigger than "time spent".
- Token elasticity of ~0.25 at the real budget means serving throughput gains translate weakly into score at the margin; the gains shown for history length (rellik13) are about *what* stays in context, not how many tokens are generated.

### Gaps
- No offline/open-weight paper since August reports a quantified fix for the early-commitment failure.
- OPINE-World full-text ablations were not retrieved (abstract only).

## Q4. What improves action efficiency on solved levels or level-1 discovery across many unseen games?

### Takeaway
Action efficiency on solved levels is nearly saturated for the dfranzen harness (2.63 points of headroom on the public 25), so efficiency tricks cannot pay much. Level-1 discovery is the lever, and the only measured help is correct affordances (UNDO, no-op guards, ACTION7 exposure, game-over/budget-bar explanations) plus scheduler policies that guarantee every game some probe time. Nothing controlled shows a gain on unseen-game level-1 discovery beyond these.

### Cited Findings
- 2.63 of 53.51 missing points are action inefficiency; 50.88 are uncleared levels. — [skarin audit](https://www.kaggle.com/code/skarin/arc-agi-3-27-80-lb-100-trajectory-audit)
- Scoring: per-level min(1.15, (human/agent)^2), level-weighted, capped by the share of level weight actually cleared, so "efficiency on early levels never buys back a level that was not cleared". UNDO counts as a scored action but does not move the step bar (copy 9 pressed UNDO 127 times in 317 actions). — [leoprovorov handbook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/leoprovorov/fable-astra-play-arc-3-handbook-harness)
- dfranzen: UNDO mostly helped one demo game (su15) but he "suspects that this might be a very important mechanic on the competition set"; game-over prompt explains budget-bar deaths. — [dfranzen WRITEUP](https://github.com/da-fr/arc-agi-3-solution/blob/main/WRITEUP.md)
- No-op detection: +55% levels excluding ft09, 21 vs 15 levels (Qwen3.6 era). — [sonpham-org/arc-3](https://github.com/sonpham-org/arc-3)
- rellik13 harness2 guarantees a bounded first probe per game (first L1 clear / 40k tokens / 1200 s) before value-rate scheduling. Unmeasured publicly. — [rellik13 harness2](https://www.kaggle.com/code/sirikilohit/arc-agi-3-duck-18-1gc-submit)
- shiiin9's D' removes "fresh games jump the queue" and prices unstarted games by the same formula, the opposite policy to rellik13's guaranteed probe. — [shiiin9 D'](https://www.kaggle.com/code/shiiin9/affectify-arc-31-54-in-a-single-sub)
- Continual Harness (frontier): action efficiency came from "discovering workable mechanics early and reusing those mechanics on later levels" with 62% of actions from saved skills. The offline analogue, function retention, is already in dfranzen. — [Continual Harness](https://sethkarten.substack.com/p/continual-harness-an-efficient-self)
- Graph-based exploration without an LLM: median 30/52 levels on 6 preview games, 3rd on the preview private LB (pre-competition era). — [arXiv 2512.24156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24156)

### Inferences
- The two public scheduler variants disagree on level-1 policy (guaranteed probe vs. no fresh-first). Neither is measured; a matched local A/B on the share of games with ≥1 level cleared is the right metric, since that is where both skarin and our headroom notes put the score.
- Best-of-N across runs is not possible inside one kernel, but within-kernel diversity (a second attempt on a game with a fresh context once the first stalls) is the nearest offline analogue. It is untested apart from rellik13's single null "restart" run.

### Gaps
- No public measurement of level-1 discovery rate on unseen games versus scheduler policy.
- No ARC-AGI-3 result for image representation changes beyond 10x upscaling (dfranzen, positive, unquantified) vs higher resolution (rellik13 and Makarov, null) vs 66-token 256 px boards (lordhansolo and rellik13, no A/B).
